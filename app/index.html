How it works

Four steps run every week for each simulated driver.

  1. HoldGig payouts land in a buffer, not straight in checking. A share is set aside for tax.
  2. ForecastLightGBM predicts a low, middle and high case for next week’s take-home pay.
  3. CalibrateConformal prediction adjusts the low case so it is right as often as it claims.
  4. PayA Monte Carlo test picks the highest salary that keeps the chance of an empty buffer under your limit.

      How Keel sets a paycheck
      1. Forecast a range. Keel estimates a low and a high case for next week's take-home pay. The wider the range, the less sure it is.
      2. Imagine many possible weeks. It plays out hundreds of possible next few weeks inside that range.
      3. Try paychecks from high to low. It starts at a cap and steps down $5 at a time.
      4. Stop at the first safe one. It keeps the first paycheck where the chance of the buffer running dry stays within your limit.

      More uncertainty (a wider range) or a smaller buffer means a lower paycheck. A bigger buffer or a looser limit means a higher one. Keel does not show which model inputs mattered most, because the results file does not record that.
